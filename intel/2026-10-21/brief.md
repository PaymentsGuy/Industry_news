## Material signals

### OCC finds restrictive debanking policies at all nine largest supervised banks

The OCC found that all nine largest national banks it supervises maintained policies or practices that could restrict or influence access to financial services based on customers’ political or religious beliefs or lawful business activities.[REF 1] The review covered activity from 2020 through September 2025 and identified broad reputation-risk and industry-specific policies that lacked sufficiently clear, objective, risk-based standards.[REF 1]

For ASA, the finding raises regulatory and trust considerations for financial-access positioning, risk-policy explainability, privacy, and controls governing customer-impacting decisions. The signal is supervisory and reputational; the supplied evidence does not establish a specific ASA obligation or enforcement action.

### TD and Akoya advance consent-driven financial-data connectivity

TD and Akoya plan to move third-party providers and aggregators onto Akoya’s Open Finance Solution through secure APIs, reducing reliance on credential-based access.[REF 2] The model is intended to let TD customers choose which apps can access their data, see who has access, and revoke access when they choose; rollout is planned in phases beginning in the fourth quarter of 2026.[REF 2]

For ASA, the announcement may affect Vault integration assumptions and raises the competitive and customer-expectation bar for consent, authorization visibility, revocation, and secure financial-data connectivity. The announcement describes plans and a phased rollout, not completed market-wide adoption.[REF 2]
