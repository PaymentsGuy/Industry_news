## Top summary

Three material signals emerged: Mahalo strengthened its Corelation distribution position; FinCEN withdrew a proposed lower cross-border transfer-recordkeeping threshold while restricting U.S. financial institutions from transacting with MEXC Global; and FinCEN’s appeal kept uncertainty alive around nationwide AML reporting for certain residential real-estate transfers. The implications for ASA are concentrated in channel access, AML/transaction-monitoring controls, and compliance-positioning assumptions.

## Material signals

### Mahalo expands within the Corelation ecosystem

Dynamic Federal Credit Union selected Mahalo’s digital-banking platform, adding another Corelation KeyStone credit union to Mahalo’s footprint.[REF 1] The development may intensify competition for ASA’s channel access and distribution strategy among Corelation institutions, particularly where native core integration is a selection criterion.[REF 1]

### FinCEN withdraws proposed threshold reduction and restricts MEXC transactions

FinCEN and the Federal Reserve withdrew a proposal that would have reduced the cross-border funds-transfer recordkeeping and Travel Rule threshold from $3,000 to $250; existing requirements remain in place.[REF 2] Separately, FinCEN prohibited covered U.S. financial institutions from processing funds transfers involving MEXC Global, reinforcing the need for adaptable AML, sanctions, transaction-monitoring, and digital-asset controls.[REF 2]

### FinCEN appeal sustains real-estate AML reporting uncertainty

FinCEN is appealing a ruling that vacated its nationwide Residential Real Estate Rule, which would require reporting for certain non-financed residential real-estate transfers involving entities or trusts.[REF 3] The rule is not currently in effect, so the appeal preserves uncertainty around future financial-crime reporting obligations and related ASA compliance and platform-positioning assumptions.[REF 3]
