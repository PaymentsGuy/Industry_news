**This week's headline:** Agentic payments and AI-native identity/fraud stacks continue to harden while OCC, FinCEN, and AML/third‑party guidance keep regulatory pressure elevated.

---

## TL;DR

1. Socure expanded both capital and customer footprint with a $156M raise at a $5.2B valuation, the Fravity acquisition, and claims of servicing four of five top U.S. crypto exchanges.[REF 8][REF 9]  
2. U.S. banking agencies proposed replacing legacy third‑party risk guidance with a principles‑based framework, and FinCEN flagged $17.5B in suspicious health‑care activity from BSA filings, underscoring rising vendor and AML scrutiny.[REF 4][REF 6]  
3. Agentic AI is moving from standards to rails and providers as Ant International and Worldline launch agentic payments protocols, and OpenAI ships a financial‑services‑specific ChatGPT.[REF 2][REF 3][REF 7]

## By roadmap area

**Vault.** No material signal this week.

**Compass.** No material signal this week.

**Auth.** Socure’s funding and Fravity acquisition strengthen an AI‑driven fraud and compliance automation stack, and its claimed penetration of four of five top U.S. crypto exchanges reinforces its position as a default identity/compliance engine for high‑risk digital assets.[REF 8][REF 9] For ASA Auth, this raises expectations that risk models are agentic, multi‑source, and tuned for complex verticals (e.g., crypto) rather than generic login/device checks.[REF 8][REF 9] Recommendation: **Monitor** Socure product and partnership disclosures for concrete feature gaps ASA should address in Auth.  

**Verify.** Building on prior Socure coverage, this week’s capital raise and Fravity buy confirm long‑term investment in automated fraud investigations rather than point‑in‑time verification only, while the crypto‑exchange footprint signals Verify‑adjacent KYC/CIP now being sold as a horizontal compliance layer.[REF 8][REF 9] Combined with FinCEN’s $17.5B suspicious‑activity finding from BSA filings, the direction of travel is toward continuous AML/KYC scrutiny with regulators expecting richer tooling.[REF 6] For ASA Verify, this supports positioning around integrated identity + AML workflows but also raises the competitive bar on sophistication and regulated‑segment proof points.[REF 6][REF 8][REF 9] Recommendation: **Investigate** how existing ASA Verify customers are handling BSA/AML pain points today and where Socure‑style capabilities are most attractive.  

**Pay.** EMVCo’s agentic framework already in the ledger is now being operationalized: Ant International launched a protocol allowing AI agents to initiate mobile payments across wallets and interfaces, and Worldline is marketing itself as among the first European providers accepting agentic AI payments.[REF 2][REF 3] These moves show “agent can pay” is becoming an infrastructural expectation, not just a standards draft, and they raise expectations that ASA Pay can talk concretely about agent‑initiated payments rather than abstract AI support.[REF 2][REF 3] Chime’s agreement to acquire sponsor bank Stride for $590M cash also reinforces a trend of fintechs pulling core banking and payments infrastructure in‑house, which could change which entities ASA integrates with for Pay and how sponsor‑bank risk is framed.[REF 11] Recommendation: **Monitor** global agentic payments launches for practical design patterns, and **Decision needed by 2026-11-01** on whether ASA Pay commits to a first agentic‑payments pilot narrative.  

**One_view.** No material signal this week.

**Forecast.** No material signal this week.

**Horizontal.** Regulators and core infrastructure continue to move: U.S. banking agencies (Fed, FDIC, OCC, NCUA) proposed replacing existing third‑party risk guidance with a principles‑based framework, explicitly reshaping how banks think about vendor risk and “vendors they cannot replace.”[REF 4] This will directly affect how ASA is diligenced and documented inside FI risk programs, nudging ASA toward clearer articulation of oversight, auditability, and substitution risk.[REF 4] FinCEN’s estimate that BSA filings point to roughly $17.5B in possible health‑care fraud is a reminder that AML focus remains high and sector‑specific, with implications for how ASA frames vertical fraud and compliance.[REF 6] OpenAI’s finance‑focused ChatGPT version raises expectations that every FI vendor offers AI‑assisted workflows for research, modeling, and client materials, but does not directly intersect ASA’s distribution partners yet.[REF 7] Recommendation: **Monitor** the third‑party risk and AML program proposals for concrete language ASA must mirror in sales and security documentation; **Investigate** whether ASA should publish a short vendor‑risk memo tailored to community banks by year‑end.
