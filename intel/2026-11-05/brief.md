## Material signals

### Multi-agent AI adoption is outpacing orchestration

Capgemini reports that 59% of financial-services firms are piloting or deploying multi-agent AI in production, while only one in ten have achieved the enterprise-wide orchestration needed for autonomous operation.[REF 1] The gap supports continued emphasis on governed agent orchestration, trusted data access, security, and measurable controls across ASA’s platform, but does not by itself require an immediate product decision.[REF 1]

### Consumers want AI help, not autonomous money management

A FIS survey of 2,000 UK adults found that 22% are ready to hand day-to-day money management to an AI assistant, while 58% are not.[REF 2] The evidence supports human-in-the-loop controls, bounded automation, and explainable recommendations in ASA Compass and Forecast rather than unrestricted autonomous execution.[REF 2]

### Jack Henry raises outlook amid stronger channel momentum

Jack Henry reported fiscal Q1 GAAP revenue growth of 8.8% and raised its fiscal 2027 revenue outlook to 6.0%–6.8% GAAP growth and 6.6%–7.4% non-GAAP growth.[REF 3] Management highlighted nine competitive core wins, 11% cloud-services revenue growth, accelerating security demand, and 42% year-over-year growth in Zelle, RTP, and FedNow payment volume.[REF 3] The combination increases competitive pressure on ASA’s channel-partner positioning and distribution strategy, particularly across cloud, payments, security, and core-connected offerings.[REF 3]
