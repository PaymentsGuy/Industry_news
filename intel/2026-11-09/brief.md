Three distinct material events emerged: Lumin launched an end-to-end M&A digital-banking conversion framework; the OCC issued an examiner framework for banks’ generative and agentic AI use; and FinCEN cut MEXC Global off from the U.S. financial system over at least $16 billion in alleged illicit flows. ## Material signals

### Lumin launches M&A digital-banking conversion framework

Lumin introduced an end-to-end framework intended to help financial institutions manage digital-banking conversions during acquisitions. The launch could strengthen Lumin’s channel-partner position and increase competitive pressure around ASA’s integration and distribution strategy.[REF 1]

### OCC issues examiner framework for bank use of generative and agentic AI

The OCC issued a supervisory framework to help examiners evaluate banks’ use of “super intelligence,” including generative and agentic AI, and the associated risks and controls.[REF 2] The framework is an examiner resource rather than a new set of enforceable bank requirements; it emphasizes applying existing governance, model-risk, technology, compliance, operational-risk, oversight, and resilience practices according to the materiality of each use case.[REF 2]

### FinCEN cuts off MEXC Global from U.S. financial system

FinCEN issued an order effectively cutting MEXC Global off from the U.S. financial system and preventing U.S. financial institutions from processing funds transfers involving the exchange.[REF 3] FinCEN said MEXC facilitated at least $16 billion in illicit transactions connected to sanctions evasion, cybercrime, and other illicit activity.[REF 3][REF 4] The action reinforces the importance of AML, sanctions-screening, transaction-monitoring, and counterparty-risk controls across ASA’s identity and financial-data platform.
